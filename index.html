Galami Labs

A workshop for new ideas

In plain words

Try a small idea first.
Keep it only if it works.

Galami Labs is Guillaume’s workshop. He uses it to test business ideas on a small budget, with real people, before spending serious time or money. Think of it as a kitchen: taste a recipe with a few guests before opening a restaurant.

How it works

  1. Listen Read what people already complain about. Then talk to them — not only online.
  2. Test cheaply Build the smallest possible version. A page, a sample, a conversation. Not a big company yet.
  3. Keep or stop If people use it or pay something, continue. If they don’t, stop and try the next idea.

There is a fixed pot of money for experiments — $10,000 for the whole workshop — so one idea cannot quietly spend everything.

What is on the bench

These are current experiments, explained the way you would at dinner.

Dorm frames

Simple picture frames for student rooms that go up without drilling holes or buying a toolbox.

Job matching

A tool that helps people find jobs they can actually get — not listings that quietly require five years of experience.

Privacy helper

A service that helps people take their personal details off the public internet.

Founders club

A small community for international students who want to start something in a new country.

Why it is set up this way

Most new businesses fail because someone builds for months before asking whether anyone wants it. The lab exists so Guillaume can be honest early: some ideas will stop. That is the point. The ones that stay should be able to stand on their own.

The legal company behind the work is Gruyters Ventures. Galami Labs is the workshop name — the place ideas are tested.

Who

Guillaume Gruyters. Belgian–Swiss. He studied business in Chicago and builds small products himself. Galami is the name he uses for this work.
